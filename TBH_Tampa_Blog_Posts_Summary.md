## Post 1: How to Stop Foreclosure in Tampa Before the Auction Date

**Category:** Foreclosure | **Read Time:** 7 min | **Date:** June 2026

### Summary

This post addresses homeowners facing foreclosure sales in Tampa. The key message is that the problem extends beyond the house itself—it involves missed mortgage payments, legal notices, and pending auctions. The goal is to choose a path that protects time, privacy, equity, and certainty.

### Key Points

- Hillsborough County foreclosure sales occur online every weekday at 10:00 a.m. by the Clerk's Office
- The mortgagor's right of redemption ends when the Certificate of Sale is issued
- Main options include: requesting reinstatement/repayment from lender, seeking loan modification or forbearance, selling before auction, or consulting a Florida attorney
- A comparison table shows four main paths: keeping the property, repairing and listing, renting, or selling as-is
- Each path has specific conditions when it makes sense and associated risks
- An as-is sale may make sense when property condition, timeline, privacy needs, or paperwork make traditional listing difficult
- The post emphasizes the importance of acting before the Certificate of Sale is issued
- Includes a practical checklist covering mortgage payoff info, title issues, carrying costs, and the need to get all terms in writing
- Contains FAQs addressing: selling before foreclosure, whether acceptance of offer stops foreclosure, handling scheduled auction dates, and when to consult an attorney

**Target Audience:** Tampa Bay homeowners in foreclosure facing auction dates

## Post 4: Behind on Mortgage Payments in Tampa? What to Do Before It Becomes a Foreclosure

**Category:** Foreclosure | **Read Time:** 5 min | **Date:** June 2026

### Summary

This post addresses Tampa homeowners who have missed or are about to miss mortgage payments. The problem involves late fees, collection calls, foreclosure fear, and uncertainty about whether to catch up, modify the loan, or sell.

### Key Points

- Core principle: the earlier the decision is made, the more options available—every missed payment narrows the window
- The critical first step is getting the exact reinstatement or payoff amount from the loan servicer in writing (without this number, it's impossible to assess options)
- Main options include: contacting the servicer for the exact amount to bring the loan current, documenting hardship and asking about retention options (reinstatement, repayment plan, forbearance, modification), calculating available equity after payoff and selling costs, and comparing traditional listing timelines against faster as-is sales
- Same four-path decision framework: keep, repair and list, rent, or sell as-is
- As-is sales may make sense when time is limited and repairs would consume the remaining window before foreclosure
- Practical checklist includes getting the exact reinstatement/payoff amount in writing, gathering property tax status, insurance, HOA info, repair issues, confirming title issues, estimating carrying costs (missed payments, fees, insurance, taxes), and comparing sale plans
- FAQs address: whether to wait for foreclosure notice (no—waiting reduces options), credit protection through selling, the most important number (payoff/reinstatement amount), repair requirements, and retention options if keeping the house
- Emphasizes that contacting the servicer about retention options early is critical

**Target Audience:** Tampa Bay homeowners facing missed mortgage payments and potential foreclosure

## Post 5: Selling a Tampa House That Needs Major Repairs

**Category:** Damage | **Read Time:** 5 min | **Date:** June 2026

### Summary

This post addresses property owners whose Tampa houses need major repairs they cannot or don't want to complete. The problem involves repair bids, contractor delays, insurance issues, failed inspections, buyer financing problems, and fear the house won't qualify for normal sale.

### Key Points

- Core principle: compare the real cost of repairs against the certainty of an as-is exit—the highest headline price isn't always best when repairs create months of delays and risk
- Critical distinction: certain repairs create major problems (roof, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, foundation, mold, water intrusion, storm damage) because lenders flag them and appraisers note them—financed buyers may be unable to close without completion. Cosmetic issues (paint, fixtures, flooring) rarely kill deals alone
- Main options include: getting realistic repair estimates (actual contractor quotes, not ballpark figures), separating safety/structural issues from cosmetic upgrades, asking agents what repairs lenders typically require, and requesting an as-is cash offer before spending money on repairs
- Same four-path decision framework: keep, repair and list, rent, or sell as-is
- As-is sales make sense when property condition, timeline, privacy needs, or paperwork complicate traditional listing
- Practical checklist includes gathering mortgage payoff info, property tax status, insurance, HOA info, repair issues, confirming title issues, estimating carrying costs, and comparing sale plans
- FAQs address: which repairs scare buyers most (roof, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, foundation, mold, water intrusion, storm damage), whether to repair before selling (only if net gain is clear), selling with unclosed permits (possible but requires disclosure), whether as-is buyers still inspect (yes, but don't require repairs), and how to compare offers (look at net proceeds, timeline, contingencies, obligations, costs, and funding certainty—not just headline price)
- Emphasizes getting an as-is offer first to inform repair decisions

**Target Audience:** Tampa Bay homeowners with properties needing major repairs they're unable or unwilling to complete
